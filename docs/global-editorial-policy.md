# Global Editorial, Monetization and Audience Policy

**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Scope:** every channel, format, workflow, asset and platform operated by this repository.

This policy is the shared operating contract for the Research Engine, Editorial Engine, Content Engine, QA, n8n orchestration, Publishing Engine and Analytics. Channel-specific settings may make controls stricter; they must not silently weaken global safeguards.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. **Viewer value before volume.** Every episode must answer a real question, tell a coherent story, explain a mechanism or provide a distinct perspective.
2. **Original editorial contribution.** A topic may be inspired by public reporting, but the episode needs its own thesis, synthesis, explanation, narrative structure or analysis. Rephrasing a source or swapping nouns in a template is not enough.
3. **Evidence traceability.** Material factual claims must be traceable to the research packet. Prefer primary sources. Track URL, publisher and publication date; identify estimates, forecasts, opinions and unresolved conflicts.
4. **Rights-aware media.** Every third-party clip, image, music track and dataset needs a rights/provenance check. Availability online does not imply permission. Reuse must be reviewed for rights and meaningful transformation.
5. **Truthful packaging.** Title, thumbnail, description and opening must make the same promise. Do not fabricate numbers, screenshots, quotes, outcomes or urgency.
6. **No interchangeable mass production.** Block exact duplicate paragraphs and flag episodes whose structure, narration or visuals are substantially interchangeable with prior episodes.
7. **Synthetic media.** Review whether realistic altered or synthetic content requires a platform disclosure. Never present generated evidence, people or events as authentic documentation.
8. **Human control.** Automated QA can block and flag; it cannot certify legal compliance or guarantee monetization. A person must review the rendered video and metadata before upload. Uploads remain private, and public release requires a separate explicit approval.

## Stage gates

- `IDEA`: topic has an audience problem/question, channel fit and proposed original angle.
- `RESEARCHED`: source packet meets the risk profile; claims and uncertainties are recorded.
- `SCRIPTED`: script contains a thesis, evidence-led development, distinct viewer payoff and a conclusion; it is not a source readout.
- `PRODUCED`: visuals support the narration and asset provenance is recorded.
- `QA_APPROVED`: automated hard checks pass; warnings are resolved or explicitly accepted by a reviewer.
- `HUMAN_REVIEWED`: reviewer watched the rendered file and checked factual accuracy, pacing, audio, captions, visual relevance, rights, metadata and disclosure.
- `UPLOADED_PRIVATE`: platform upload succeeded with private visibility.
- `PUBLIC_APPROVED`: a separate human action approved public release.
- `PUBLISHED`: platform state and URL are recorded.
- `MEASURED`: metrics and production cost are captured for learning.

A failed gate returns the episode to the relevant earlier state. Never auto-advance from QA to public publication.

## Research and risk

Standard-risk topics require at least two traceable sources; high-risk topics require at least three and stronger primary-source support. High-risk subjects include finance/markets, health, legal matters, public safety and consequential claims about named people or organizations. Distinguish fact, estimate, forecast and interpretation. Financial content must not promise returns or issue individualized buy/sell instructions.

## Audience and discovery

Choose topics using audience relevance, evidence strength, novelty, visual potential, hook potential and production feasibility. Search intent, recommendations and returning-viewer value are different jobs; do not optimize all episodes for the same metric. The title and thumbnail should communicate the actual value, not merely maximize clicks. Judge CTR alongside traffic source, impressions and retention.

## Analytics loop

Track impressions, CTR, views, watch time, average view duration, retention, returning viewers, subscribers gained, RPM, estimated revenue and production cost where available. Compare videos with similar format, length and traffic mix. Do not rewrite channel strategy based on a single outlier; use at least five measured episodes as the minimum directional sample, then continue testing.
